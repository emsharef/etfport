# Rebalancing a portfolio chosen under the old beliefs

Saved before computation, 2026-10-02. User-requested follow-up to the diversified example. Model version: M9. This is numerical analysis of the existing model, not new theory or an empirical backtest. Existing studies remain unchanged.

## Question

When a manager revises forecasts, how do one-review and funded two-review optimization adjust a portfolio that was already optimal under the old beliefs? Does the result survive removing the arbitrary starting allocation? Distinguish current forecast revisions from predictable future changes. An unchanged-beliefs control must be reported even if it is the main finding.

## Starting portfolio and controls

Use the six-fund, three-ETF opportunity set specified in ../diversified/DESIGN.md and menu.py, with the same fees, loadings, return risks and state dynamics. Initial wealth is one. For each old-belief parameter configuration, choose the long-only, funded, uncapped one-quarter optimum with investor transaction costs removed. Treat its acquisition costs as sunk. Carry those holdings into the comparison; do not generate a starting portfolio using the revised forecasts. This is an assumed already-held target portfolio, not a simulated trading history or an optimal infinite-horizon portfolio.

Before any revised-forecast comparison, verify that the one-review optimizer with the actual trading costs makes no material trade under unchanged beliefs. Independently reproduce the old optimum with SLSQP. No past realized-return marking is introduced: the exercise conditions on an already-held portfolio and isolates a forecast change. Future marking, uncertainty, learning and funding remain in the existing M9 tree. Forecast revisions are conditional input scenarios, not claimed to have been generated by the model's observation filter.

## Nine forecast scenarios

Old sleeve premia are (120,30,60) bp per quarter. Old fund alphas are (4,2,1,0.5,3,1) bp, subject to the explicitly specified negative-alpha sensitivity below. All mean persistence coefficients remain 0.8 and state-noise variances remain fixed.

1. Unchanged: current and long-run means remain at the old values.
2–3. Persistent premium revision, either sign: current sleeve premia change by (-15,+5,0) bp or its negative. Set long-run means to those revised values, so the expected next-quarter means stay there.
4–5. Reverting premium revision, either sign: the same current changes, but keep old long-run means. The expected next-review deviation is 80% of today's revision.
6–7. Persistent manager revision: Equity A's current and long-run alpha change by +5 bp or -5 bp. Other mean forecasts do not change.
8–9. Anticipated premium change, either sign: current means are unchanged; conditional next-review sleeve means change by (-15,+5,0) bp or its negative. Set long-run means to old means plus five times the desired next-review change. This is a two-review illustration, not a calibrated long-run forecast.

Map sleeve premium changes to primitive factors with the inverse ETF loading matrix. Hold belief variances fixed within each before/after comparison; do not conflate a changed estimate with greater confidence in it.

## Policies, metrics and sample

Use exactly the existing 432-state finite axis law and public observations. The filter is linear, not the finite law's exact posterior. Compare the one-review root followed by funded optimal continuation, the full funded two-review optimum, and the ETF-only one-review root followed by full funded continuation. ETF-only freezes fund dollar positions today only. No policy may borrow at either review. The objective is the existing additive two-review mean-variance score, not terminal utility or realized investment performance.

Record initial holdings, root holdings and trades by instrument, cash, funding and cost identities, sleeve exposure changes, current and expected continuation scores, expected next-review trades and costs, and each policy's common-horizon value. Report the one-review score improvement from allowing fund trades separately from the planning gain. Report the planning gain minus its matched unchanged-beliefs control as a descriptive comparison, not an additive causal decomposition. The headline case is the persistent downward equity/upward duration revision at baseline inputs, chosen before results.

108 cases: nine forecast scenarios crossed with six baseline-cost belief/risk configurations (gamma 2.5 with both premium and alpha prior SD scales 0.5,1,2; gamma 2 and 3 with scale 1; and gamma 2.5, scale 1 with every old alpha reduced by 5 bp, making all old net alphas negative). Baseline fund and ETF costs are 5 and 2 bp each way. At the baseline positive-alpha configuration, run nine scenarios for six additional cost pairs in bp: (0,0), (0,2), (20,2), (5,0), (5,5), (20,5). All costs and forecasts are assumed. No concentration caps or minimum allocations.

Use deterministic exact finite-law sums, so no Monte Carlo standard error or random seed is applicable. Save all results, environment and code hashes. Check nonnegative cash and holdings to 2e-7, root funding to 1e-9, and reconstructed objective to 1e-8; distinguish numerical zero from meaningful trades. Use an independent vectorized QP, directly constructed moments, filter and state law, to reproduce all nine baseline comparisons. Require gain agreement within 1e-5 bp and holding agreement within 1e-5 of initial wealth. Report warnings and deviations. A result with no important planning gain, unchanged ETF direction, or no fund trading is a valid finding.

## Scope

This removes a poor initial allocation as an explanation for the one-review response. It does not remove all reasons for dynamic trading under unchanged means: future returns change dollar holdings, the filter changes beliefs and predictive covariance, and future budgets and costs still matter. No claim of a stable long-run allocation or an empirical calibration. No new Lean theorem or model change.
